## 2. Tiny Habits by BJ Fogg - Core Message
**Question:** How can you use B = MAP to make making new habits easier? What are M, A, and P?  
**Ans.:** B = MAP means a behavior happens when Motivation, Ability, and a Prompt come together. To make habits easier, shrink them to under 30 seconds, anchor them to an existing routine, and celebrate each tiny win so motivation grows.

**Question:** Why is it important to "Shine" or celebrate after each successful completion of a habit?  
**Ans.:** Celebrating right after a tiny win creates "success momentum": the good feeling builds your confidence and motivation, so you're more likely to repeat the habit and gradually grow it.

## 4. Book Summary of Atomic Habits
**Question:** What is the book's perspective about Identity?  
**Ans.:** The book treats identity as the "North Star" of habit change: profound change comes from shifting your beliefs about who you are, not just from chasing outcomes like weight loss or a deployment.  

**Question:** Write about the book's perspective on how to make a habit easier to do?   
**Ans.:** The third rule in the book is "make it easy," which means cut down the hassle and set up your space so the habit is ready to go. The speaker in the video claims that friction is the most powerful force in the universe, and anything that helps the habit change, no matter how small, is going to play a big role. 

**Question:** Write about the book's perspective on how to make a habit harder to do?   
**Ans.:** To make a bad habit harder, flip the four laws: make it invisible, unattractive, difficult, and unsatisfying. In practice, that means removing the cues that trigger it and adding extra steps between you and the behavior.  

## 5. Reflection

**Question:** Pick one habit that you would like to do more of? What are the steps that you can take to make it make the cue obvious or the habit more attractive or easy and/or response satisfying?  
**Ans.:**  I pick a habit to spend my days in a more structured way where I don't do random meaningless stuff. I'll plan tomorrow the night before and tie it to a fixed cue, like reviewing my plan after breakfast. Start with just my first three tasks, use a planner I enjoy, and tick off each one so finishing feels rewarding.

**Question:** Pick one habit that you would like to eliminate or do less of? What are the steps that you can take to make it make the cue invisible, the process unattractive or hard, or the response unsatisfying?  
**Ans.:**  To cut down on doomscrolling, I'll make the cue invisible by charging my phone across the room, hiding the apps in a folder, and turning off notifications. I'll add friction with app timers or logging out after each use, and remind myself that it leaves me feeling worse, so the habit becomes less appealing and less rewarding.

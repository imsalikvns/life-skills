# Listening and Active Communication

## 1. Active Listening
**Question:** What are the steps/strategies for active listening?  
**Ans.:** The following are the steps for active listening:
1. Do not get distracted by your own thoughts.
2. Focus on the speaker and the topic being discussed.
3. Do not interrupt. Let them finish.
4. Maintain positive body language.
5. Take notes wherever necessary.
6. Repeat to make sure you understood what has been discussed.

## 2. Reflective Listening
**Question:** According to Fisher's model, what are the key points of Reflective Listening?  
**Ans.:** Fisher's model has the following points:
1. Orientation: Stakeholders break the ice and get to know each other so they don't feel hesitant while sharing their views.
2. Conflict: The members (stakeholders in the communication) push for their views to be accepted.
3. Emergence: The members try to listen and understand others' opinions and begin to form loose agreement.
4. Reinforcement: The group reaches a decision.

## 3. Reflection
**Question:** What are the obstacles in your listening process?  
**Ans.:** My own thoughts are the major obstacle in my listening. I listen to a point, then my brain picks it and thinks about it while losing the next things the speaker is saying.

**Question:** What can you do to improve your listening?  
**Ans.:** I should take notes on the points my mind thinks about mid-conversation and discuss those points when the speaker is done speaking. Rather than letting them distract me.

## 4. Types of Communication
**Question:** When do you switch to a passive communication style in your day-to-day life?  
**Ans.:** When I face a situation where I think I'm not entitled to get a certain response/treatment from the other person, I turn passive.  

**Question:** When do you switch into Aggressive communication styles in your day-to-day life?  
**Ans.:** When someone violates a very basic expected behavior, I switch to aggressive communication. 

**Question:** When do you switch into passive-aggressive (sarcasm/gossiping/taunts/silent treatment and others) communication styles in your day-to-day life?  
**Ans.:** This usually doesn't happen. But sometimes when I'm around someone familiar, and the situation demands (or I think it demands), I switch to a passive-aggressive style.

**Question:** How can you make your communication assertive?  
**Ans.:** I can channel my thoughts to better understand what I want (a day off to recover from burnout or better working conditions). I should sound commanding (with empathy and respect) when communicating about my legit needs.
